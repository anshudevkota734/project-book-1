CHAPTER 8: CYBER LAW AND COMPUTER ETHICS

8.1 Digital Society and Computer Ethics

Digital Society: A modern society integrated with information and communication technology (ICT) where daily activities, communications, education, commerce, and governance occur online.
Computer Ethics: A set of moral principles and standard practices governing the responsible use of computers, digital devices, and networks.

10 Commandments of Computer Ethics (CEI):
• Do not use a computer to harm other people.
• Do not interfere with other people's computer work.
• Do not snoop around in other people's computer files.
• Do not use a computer to steal.
• Do not use a computer to bear false witness.
• Do not copy or use proprietary software without paying.
• Do not use other people's computer resources without authorization.
• Do not appropriate other people's intellectual output.
• Think about the social consequences of the program you are writing.
• Use a computer in ways that ensure consideration and respect for others.

8.2 Concept of Information Security

Information Security (InfoSec) refers to the practices and tools designed to protect digital and physical information from unauthorized access, use, disclosure, disruption, modification, or destruction.

Core Principles — CIA Triad:
• Confidentiality: Ensuring data is accessible only to authorized users (achieved via encryption, access control).
• Integrity: Maintaining the accuracy and completeness of data by preventing unauthorized alterations (achieved via hashing, digital signatures).
• Availability: Ensuring information and resources are accessible to authorized users when needed (achieved via backups, redundant systems).

8.3 Concept of Cybercrime

Any illegal activity carried out using a computer, network, or networked device as a primary tool or target.

Types of Cybercrime:
• Hacking: Unauthorized entry into a computer network or system.
• Phishing: Deceptive communication (emails, messages) designed to trick users into revealing sensitive information like passwords or credit card numbers.
• Cyberbullying / Online Harassment: Using digital channels to intimidate, defame, or distress an individual.
• Identity Theft: Stealing personal identity data to commit fraud.
• Software Piracy: Unauthorized copying, distribution, or use of copyrighted software.

8.4 Malicious Software (Malware) and Spam

Malware: Short for "malicious software"—programs explicitly written to harm, exploit, or compromise computer systems.
• Virus: A self-replicating program that attaches itself to executable files and spreads when executed.
• Worm: Standalone malware that self-replicates across networks without needing a host file.
• Trojan Horse: Destructive software disguised as legitimate or useful software.
• Ransomware: Encrypts victim data and demands payment (ransom) to restore access.
• Spyware: Secretly monitors user activities, keystrokes, and personal data.

Spam: Unsolicited bulk electronic messages sent primarily over email for commercial, promotional, or malicious intent.

8.5 Protection from Cybercrime

• Firewalls: Hardware or software boundaries that monitor and filter incoming and outgoing network traffic.
• Antivirus & Anti-malware Software: Applications designed to scan, detect, quarantine, and eliminate malicious code.
• Data Backup: Regularly copying essential files to isolated external media or secure cloud platforms.
• Strong Passwords & MFA: Utilizing complex passphrases and Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA/2FA) for accounts.
• Software Updates / Patching: Keeping operating systems and applications updated to fix security vulnerabilities.

8.6 Intellectual Property Rights (IPR)

Legal rights granted to individuals or organizations over the creations of their minds (literary works, artistic creations, inventions, logos, software).

• Copyright: Protects original literary, artistic, and software code creations against unauthorized copying.
• Patent: Exclusive rights granted for inventions, offering protection over technological solutions or processes.
• Trademark: Protects recognizable signs, logos, names, or slogans used to distinguish goods or services.
• Trade Secrets: Protection of confidential business methods, formulas, or strategies.

8.7 Concept of Digital Signature

A Digital Signature is a mathematical cryptographic mechanism used to verify the authenticity, integrity, and non-repudiation of a digital document or communication.

Working Principle:
• Utilizes Asymmetric Cryptography (Public Key Infrastructure - PKI).
• The sender generates a unique hash value of the message and encrypts it using their Private Key to produce the signature.
• The receiver decrypts the signature using the sender's Public Key and compares the hash to verify authenticity and check for tampering.

Key Properties: Authenticity (verifies sender), Integrity (proves message wasn't altered), Non-repudiation (sender cannot deny sending the message).

8.8 Concept of Cyber Law in Nepal

Cyber Law in Nepal refers to the legal framework established to regulate internet use, electronic transactions, digital signatures, and punish computer-related crimes.

Primary Legislation: Electronic Transaction Act (ETA), 2063 BS (2006 AD).

Key Objectives of ETA 2063:
• To grant legal recognition and validity to electronic records and digital signatures.
• To establish the Office of the Controller of Certifying Authorities (OCCA) to oversee digital certificate authorities.
• To establish an IT Tribunal to hear digital disputes and cyber offense cases.

Major Cyber Offenses & Penalties defined in Chapter 9 (Sections 44–58):
• Unauthorized access / Hacking (Sec 45): Up to NPR 200,000 fine, 3 years imprisonment, or both.
• Computer/Data damage (Sec 46): Up to NPR 200,000 fine, 3 years imprisonment, or both.
• Publication of illegal/obscene material (Sec 47): Up to NPR 100,000 fine, 5 years imprisonment, or both.
• Computer Source Code Piracy (Sec 44): Up to NPR 200,000 fine, 3 years imprisonment, or both.

8.9 ICT Policy in Nepal

Nepal's Information and Communication Technology (ICT) Policy, 2072 BS (2015 AD) outlines the national framework for leveraging technology to drive socio-economic growth, digital governance, and technical infrastructure.

Core Vision: Transform Nepal into an information- and knowledge-based society and economy.

Key Objectives:
• Facilitate and promote the use of ICT in e-governance to increase public service transparency and efficiency.
• Develop physical telecommunication and broadband infrastructure to bridge the digital divide in rural areas.
• Integrate ICT in education to develop skilled human resources in IT and computing.
• Promote free and open-source software (FOSS) and encourage local technological innovation/startups.
• Build a secure digital infrastructure by establishing cybersecurity responses like national Cyber Emergency Response Teams (N-CERT).
