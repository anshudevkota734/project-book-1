CHAPTER 1: COMPUTER SYSTEM

1.1 Introduction of Computer

A computer is an electronic device that takes raw data as input, processes it using stored instructions, and outputs meaningful information. Its main characteristics are high speed, high accuracy, immense storage, and diligence (working continuously without getting tired). However, it operates strictly on logic—meaning incorrect input inevitably produces incorrect output (GIGO: Garbage In, Garbage Out). Computer hardware evolved across five distinct generations: 1st Gen (1940s–50s): Used vacuum tubes; massive in size, generated intense heat, and used basic machine language. 2nd Gen (1950s–60s): Replaced tubes with transistors; smaller, faster, and introduced assembly/early high-level languages like FORTRAN. 3rd Gen (1960s–70s): Introduced Integrated Circuits (ICs); enabled keyboard/monitor interaction and early operating systems. 4th Gen (1970s–Present): Powered by VLSI/ULSI microprocessors; created personal computers (PCs), laptops, and microchips. 5th Gen (Present & Beyond): Focused on Artificial Intelligence (AI), parallel processing, and quantum computing for problem-solving. System speeds are measured by clock frequency in Hertz (MHz/GHz) or instruction counts (MIPS). Storage measures capacity using binary units: a bit (0 or 1), byte (8 bits), Kilobyte (1024 bytes), Megabyte (1024 KB), Gigabyte (1024 MB), and Terabyte (1024 GB). Computers are categorized by size and computational power: Supercomputers: Ultra-fast, expensive multi-processor systems built for intense scientific work (weather modeling, physics research). Mainframe Computers: Large enterprise systems designed to handle millions of simultaneous transactions securely (banks, airlines). Minicomputers: Mid-sized multi-user servers used by medium organizations. Microcomputers: Standard single-user devices like desktops, laptops, and tablets. Mobile computing allows users to access data wirelessly from portable devices without being tied to a desk. It relies on portable hardware, mobile OS platforms, and wireless networks to power modern everyday services like mobile banking (eSewa/Khalti), GPS navigation, ride-sharing, and online learning.
